Most of us are carrying more than we let on, and noticing that isn't a weakness. It's the first step.

For our last event of the semester, we held our first fully independent pop-up outside the library on the final day of classes, and we sold out. Finals season has a way of making constant productivity feel like the norm, so we handed out a guide on stress: what it is, what it does, and a few simple ways to calm your nervous system. The food gathered people; the conversation was the point.

You've heard plenty about stress. We're here to emphasize it one more time, because its impact is more serious than we often realize, and more manageable than it feels.

<div class="info-cards">
<div class="info-card">
<h3>What is stress?</h3>
<p>Stress is your body's natural response to pressure, challenge, or change. Especially during finals season it can feel overwhelming, but understanding it is the first step to managing it. Short-term stress is normal; it's when stress piles up without relief that it starts to affect everything else.</p>
</div>
<div class="info-card">
<h3>The signs</h3>
<p>Physically: headaches, muscle tension, fatigue, changes in appetite, trouble sleeping. Mentally and emotionally: anxiety, irritability, trouble concentrating, feeling overwhelmed, or losing motivation. These are valid and manageable. Left unaddressed, chronic stress can weaken your immune system and contribute to burnout, but small daily habits make a big difference over time.</p>
</div>
<div class="info-card">
<h3>A few things that genuinely help</h3>
<p>Breathing exercises (this one genuinely shocked us). An outer-loop walk reset: spot the mulberry trees. And instead of scrolling, watch something that creates awe: a nature time-lapse or space footage.</p>
</div>

## You are not alone

At Rice, we're surrounded by incredibly driven, talented people, and that also means stress and the pressure to perform can quietly become the norm. The truth is, most of us are carrying more than we let on. Recognizing that in yourself isn't a weakness; it's the first and most important step. Because when we start to see it in ourselves, we get better at seeing it in each other.

We put this together because we believe community starts with being real, and that destigmatizing mental health begins with small moments. So we hope this gives you something to think about. And we really hope you enjoyed your baked good too.
